## Supplemental Figures

Figure S1: Climate velocity projections for (a) SSP2-2050, (b) SSP5-2050, (c) SSP2-2080, and (d) SSP5-2080 at 1km resolution from Carroll 2023. Climate velocity refers to the rate at which a species must move across the landscape to maintain the same climatic conditions, calculated as the distance to a location with a similar projected climate in the target year, divided by the number of years from the baseline year or 2015. Colors represent percentiles of climate velocity values binned over all four maps (i.e., the color yellow in each map corresponds to the same climate velocity value). Climate velocity is forecasted to be highest along the Gulf Coast, in Northern portions of the study region, and in montane areas, particularly along the Appalachian Ridge, in all time periods and climate scenarios we examined. Areas with the lowest climate velocity include the Atlantic coastal regions and the foothills of the Southern Appalachians. By 2050, average climate velocity is expected to be 25.3% higher for SSP5 than for SSP2, and, by 2080, the difference is expected to widen to 58.3%, representing an average velocity of 4.21 km/year for SSP5 compared to 2.66 km/y for SSP2. 

Figure S2: Land-cover classification projections for (a) SSP2-2050, (b) SSP5-2050, (c) SSP2-2080, and (d) SSP5-2080 at 1km resolution from Chen et al. 2022. Land is classified into six land cover types in the Northeast study region including water, forest, grassland, barren, cropland, and urban cover. In the four SSP-RCP scenarios we evaluate, 27-33% of cells are expected to change in land cover type. The highest rates of land cover change are projected for the Northeastern urban corridor, followed by peninsular Florida and the Great Lakes regions of New York and Ohio.

Figure S11: Spatial correlation between concentrations of species with high exposure to climate and to land cover change for SSP5-2080. Graph is created by overlaying the community-wide exposure for CEI and LEI and calculating the degree of correlation between the relative exposure (percentile values). X- and Y- axes denote the percentile of relative exposure compared to other areas, and the colors of bins on the plot bins represent the density (in percentile) of 3 km grid blocks which share that exposure percentile for both threats. Pearson’s correlation coefficient = 0.81, and ranges between 0.80-0.84 for each of the other three scenarios.
